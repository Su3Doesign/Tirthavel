Foreword

Where it started

Four pictures saved on my phone: a stone ring, a city on a lake, temples on a cliff, an eclipse. They had nothing to do with each other. I couldn’t stop looking at them anyway.

So I tried to turn them into one place. This book is that place, and every mistake I made on the way there.

Sumanth

p.s. yes, I rendered thirty frames of a brown wall. it’s in chapter VIII.

Why a Ring?

Everyone asks. For a long time my answer was “it looked cool”.

(it still looks cool.)

II · 2

Why it won

1

You can’t miss it

The city is all towers and spikes. A circle is the one shape your eye can’t ignore.

2

Time is round

Clocks, calendars, sun wheels. People have drawn time as a circle forever.

3

It holds the sky

Whatever is behind it becomes part of it: dawn, clouds, the eclipse.

4

It works near and far

From the sea it’s a simple circle. Up close it’s covered in carvings.

5

A door in a clock

Once the Ring was a clock, its door couldn’t just lead to the other side of the hill. It had to lead to another time. The whole story grew from that.

Interlude

Echoes

प्रतिध्वनिpratidhvani · an echo

I thought I was making up a myth. Halfway through, I realised I’d grown up with it.

(the Mahabharata. it’s always the Mahabharata.)

the Ring’s name comes from this line.
kāla means time.

कालोऽस्मि लोकक्षयकृत् प्रवृद्धोलोकान् समाहर्तुम् इह प्रवृत्तः

kālo’smi loka-kṣaya-kṛt pravṛddho / lokān samāhartum iha pravṛttaḥ

“I am Time, the destroyer of worlds.”

Bhagavad Gītā 11.32

Krishna to Arjuna, just before the war.
(yes, the line Oppenheimer quoted.)

Interlude · 1

Eight petals, eight echoes

The lotus in the Ring has eight petals. Each one hides a story I borrowed.

tap a petal, or the centre

1संवत्सरचक्र

The wheel of the year

Ādi Parva

The sage Utanka sees a wheel with twelve spokes, turned by six boys. It’s the year and its six seasons.

→ a wheel that counts time, with a horse beside it. just like my reference.

2इला

Ila, mother of the moon’s line

Ādi Parva

Ila is the mother of the moon’s family line: the line both the Pandavas and the Kauravas come from.

→ so my astronomer is Ilaa, and her Ring counts the moon. 30 spokes, one per lunar day.

3चतुर्युग

The four ages

Vana Parva

Hanuman tells Bhima about the four ages of the world. Each one is shorter and darker than the last.

→ four rings of knots, one per age. the ivy has reached the last one.

4मयसभा

Maya’s hall

Sabhā Parva

In the palace Maya built, Duryodhana mistakes floor for water, water for floor, and walks straight into a door that only looked open.

→ my door does the opposite. it looks empty, and it isn’t.

5चक्रव्यूह

The wheel formation

Droṇa Parva

Abhimanyu knows how to break into the wheel formation, but not how to get out. He goes in anyway.

→ his wheel trapped his body. mine takes your shadow.

6जयद्रथवध

The sun that set early

Droṇa Parva

To save Arjuna’s promise, Krishna hides the sun, and the day ends an hour early.

→ a disc over the sun, and one moment that changes everything. that’s my Hollow Sun.

7रथाङ्गपाणि

The wheel in his hand

Bhīṣma Parva

Krishna swore he wouldn’t fight. Then he jumps off the chariot and runs at Bhishma with a wheel in his hand.

→ every wheel in the epic is moving. mine stands still and waits.

8कर्ण

The wheel the earth took

Karṇa Parva

At the worst possible moment, Karna’s chariot wheel sinks into the ground.

→ the Ring’s feet are buried in the mountain. I noticed it late and kept it.

◯सुदर्शन

The centre: Sudarshana

Krishna’s discus

Krishna’s spinning discus. Its name means “beautiful to look at”.

→ beautiful first, dangerous second. that’s the whole Ring.

These are the stories the way I heard them growing up. Any mistakes are mine.

Afterword

What I learned

Scale is a feeling. The Ring only felt huge once there were tiny people and tiny doors under it.

Rules beat decoration. Moss where water sits. Nothing in front of the Ring. Every rule made it more real.

Keep your mistakes. I remember every one in this book.

The door stays shut. People ask what’s on the other side. Some things are better as questions.
